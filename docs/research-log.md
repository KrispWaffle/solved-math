## Packing cutoff

**Question.** In §7, item 8 of Part I, the authors ask whether five occurrences suffice to distinguish any two inequivalent packing sequences. Their hypotheses allow overlaps longer than half the pattern length. Their definition prescribes window starts and permits other occurrences. The present theorem answers the question about a universal cutoff and supplies a linear lower bound along a family for the length-dependent variant. [Part I, §§2, 3, 7](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29846v1)

**Derivation.** Two increasing rows seen through sliding windows give a Catalan count. A third increasing row makes two Catalan choices share a middle chain. I tracked the gaps in that chain where both outer rows could insert entries. There is no such gap until the window count exceeds its width in columns. At the first possible overlap, the extra choice is the order of exactly two entries. A fourth row allows the comparison pattern to have exactly the same length and overlap.

**Prior-work check.** I searched by arXiv identifier and author names, and for “five occurrences”, “cutoff”, “Wilf equivalence”, and “prescribed pattern packings”. OpenAlex returned Nagy's double-factorial paper and the authors' Part II as citations of Part I. Semantic Scholar returned Nagy's paper. Part II concerns moment sequences. A separate project following the Part I problems explicitly distinguished its packing-maximum result from the unresolved cutoff question. That search missed Cinna Davis’s November 26 proof of the same negative answer. The earlier construction uses patterns of length $3L+1$ and the construction here uses length $4L$. [Earlier proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/2e54a945aa14bb6965f88f9fc21c63e1d9183921/results/blitvic-kammoun-steingrimsson-packing-cutoff/PROOF.md). [OpenAlex citation record](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W7223210855&select=title,doi) · [Part II](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12829) · [Project's item-by-item account](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Library/PermutationPatterns/blitvickammounsteingrimsson2026patterns.html)

**Check.** The program forms each actual permutation pattern, reads off the rank inequalities of every prescribed window, and recursively assigns the next smallest rank to an eligible position. It recovers the predicted counts for both patterns for $2\le k\le9$ and $1\le j\le k+1$. At $k=5$ the first difference is $19188-17424=1764$. The proof identifies this difference as $C_k^2$ for every $k$. [Proof](../archive/packing-cutoff/PROOF.md) · [Checker](../archive/packing-cutoff/verify.py)

## Big descents

**Conjecture.** Conjecture 4.9 of Elizalde, Rivera Jr., and Zhuang is a separately numbered assertion in the published article. It concerns the full quasisymmetric function built from the positions of the big descents, for every permutation length and every forbidden increasing-pattern length. The article already proves the unrestricted symmetry and positivity statement in Theorem 4.8. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00026-026-00805-1) · [Conjecture and tables in the author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.15111v2#S4.SS3)

**Derivation.** I first expanded a fundamental function as colorings and merged descending runs of consecutive values inside each constant-color block. This preserves the longest increasing subsequence and replaces big descents by ordinary descents. It proves symmetry via Robinson–Schensted and the substitution $x_i\mapsto x_i/(1-x_i)$. Symmetry alone leaves a genuine sign problem. For example, the degree-two term of $s_{(1)}$ after this substitution is $\sum_i x_i^2=s_{(2)}-s_{(1,1)}$.

I changed basis before making the substitution. Lenart's expansion preserves the first-row length, so restricting the longest increasing subsequence is compatible with that change of basis. In the dual basis, extracting squarefree coefficients leaves fillings whose equal entries occupy single vertical intervals. Marking and unmarking successive entries in a column cancels their signs. The remaining coefficients count ordinary standard tableaux. The substitution then transposes the signless Grothendieck functions under the usual Schur involution, giving the final positive formula. [Lenart's formula in Buch](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0004137) · [Lam–Pylyavskyy duality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0705.2189) · [Yeliussizov's substitution and duality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.01581)

**Prior-work check.** I searched the authors' names with “Conjecture 4.9”, “big descent sets”, “Schur positivity”, “increasing pattern”, “Grothendieck”, and related terms for restricted Smirnov words. I checked the published conjecture and its preprint, then queried the citing works for both the DOI and the arXiv record. The OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar results pointed to work on peak functions and on parking-function inversions. That search missed Cinna Davis’s November 26 proof, including the refinement for an exact longest increasing subsequence length. [Earlier proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/16ac09a1dd4f9fa30b1e115c163baf4bc04083a7/results/erz-big-descent-schur/PROOF.md). [OpenAlex citing works](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W7127077845&select=title,doi) · [Pattern-Avoiding Peak Functions](https://doi.org/10.37236/14799) · [Inversions in parking functions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11587)

**Check.** I compared the proof's tableau formula with a direct enumeration of every permutation for $0\le n\le9$. The two calculations agree at each exact longest increasing subsequence length and each individual fundamental-basis index. This tests the entire function, including the orientation of descents, the transpose on the flagged filling, and the boundary cases. The general result rests on the bijection, cancellation, and cited identities in the proof. The manuscript has not undergone independent peer review. [Proof](../archive/big-descents/PROOF.md) · [Checker](../archive/big-descents/verify.py)

## 27 November 2026 — Pattern-poset separators

Section 7, item 5 proposes a specific separator for all patterns with the given boundary ranks. I started with the two shared positions. Comparing the proposed separator with later windows reduces to comparing it with each window’s minimum. The harder step is proving that two positions between successive shared ranks stay incomparable when further windows are attached.

Giving the two positions equal height solves that problem if every window inequality strictly increases height. The overlap-two construction suggested how to handle arbitrary overlaps. At a shared boundary, adding its prefix and suffix ranks aligns the two copies. Between consecutive boundaries, a running maximum of the matching ranks extends the assignment to all positions. This gives one height function on the whole stack. Lean proves its strict increase, the two required incomparable pairs and the unrestricted occurrence count. The statement and prior-work search are recorded with the result. [Proof](../results/pattern-poset-separator/PROOF.md) · [Audit](../results/pattern-poset-separator/AUDIT.md)

## 27 November 2026 — General separator classification

Each rank in one window generates a final rank interval in the next. Compatibility makes the interval agree on every shared physical position, even across long overlaps. Iterating the two boundary maps gives an exact reachability test. Their orbits stabilize on a finite chain. The extra step for separators was to preserve three positions at once. Capping the two gaps on either side of a chosen center preserves both witnesses to a nonchain. This gives a cutoff depending only on the pattern length, and an executable test for the full question. ## 27 November 2026 — Lee–Sah height conjecture

While working on the overlap-one packing moment problem, I compared each window’s weight with beta factors at all its intermediate spine points. Lee and Sah’s Theorem 1.4 already gives the resulting exponential growth rate for the counts. The same comparison controls rare events and resolves the separate height conjecture at the end of their paper. The clean estimate comes from leaving the first point uniform, sorting the other points under the beta law, and bounding the lost mass by a polynomial in the chain length. ## 28 November 2026 — Distinct patterns in a random permutation

Conditioning on one subset turns repeated patterns into partial bijections preserving two independent orders. Decomposing a bijection into paths shows that every typical repetition comes from local replacements. Short pairs in the permutation plot then give the fluctuation law. The earlier near-perfect-packing conjecture had already been disproved. The new results answer the subsequent exact-mean and limit-law questions. [Mean proof](../results/distinct-random-permutation-patterns/PROOF.md) · [Prior work and review](../results/distinct-random-permutation-patterns/AUDIT.md)

Coupling all subset sizes by the initial segments of one random ordering gives a uniform limit for the entire normalized length profile. The sparse end uses one family of allowable exchanges across each interval of sizes from $s$ to $2s$. At the other end a single bond count predicts the scaled loss for every positive sublinear deletion sequence. [Joint profile](../results/distinct-random-permutation-patterns/UNIFORM-PROFILE.md) · [Deletion deficits](../results/distinct-random-permutation-patterns/EDGE-LENGTHS.md)

## 28 November 2026 — Folded linear forests

The paper’s journal and preprint versions assign different meanings to hardness. For the journal version, a minimal hereditary hard class must equal the hereditary hull of the output of some SAT reduction. The degree test on the binary fold made the irrational slope recoverable from long paths and supplied too many different classes for this to hold universally. A second argument diagonalizes among finite slope intervals to obtain a decidable example.
