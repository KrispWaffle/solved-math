# Prior work and review

## Opening search, 28 November 2026

The target is the bipartite-permutation case of the induced-minor well-quasi-ordering question, stated independently as Open Problem 2 in §3 of Alecu, Lozin and Malyshev. Induced minors allow vertex deletion and edge contraction. Every pair in the sequence must be compared by this relation, even if intermediate graphs leave the class. An infinite antichain answers the question in the negative. The authors point out that the familiar double-ended forks form a chain under these contractions. [Journal statement](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23011) · [Lozin and Mayhill on the two graph classes](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11083-010-9188-7)

Searches covered the original names, proper interval bigraphs, unit interval bigraphs, monotone graphs, induced minors, induced contractions, bounded-degree path blowups, distance-hereditary graphs and known induced-minor antichains. Błasiok, Kamiński, Raymond and Trunck describe several earlier antichains, including antiholes, interval graphs, the Matoušek–Nešetřil–Thomas family and nested lozenges. Their dichotomy concerns the full class excluding a single induced minor. We are checking the drawings and their actual graph classes as well as subsequent citations. [Antichain constructions, §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.07135#page=9)

The question asks whether contractions restore a classical ordering theorem inside a geometrically defined class. The proposed attack replaces path vertices by small sets of independent twins and studies which of these sets can collapse in an induced-minor model. The intended conclusion covers the entire bipartite permutation class. The unit interval graph question is separate.

## Repeat search and attribution, 28 November 2026

We repeated the search by problem number, author names, DOI, induced minor and induced contraction, proper interval bigraph, double-ended fork, nested lozenge, path blowup, bounded bandwidth and better-quasi-ordering. We also inspected the repository catalog, local and remote research refs, its issues and PRs, the public reference catalog and pinned Mathlib. We found no earlier resolution of this precise open problem. The authors explicitly distinguish the full induced-minor relation from induced subgraph containment. The 2020 account of Liu, §3.2, Conjecture 1 attributes the joint bipartite-permutation and unit interval conjecture to Lozin and Mayhill. The available institutional record of the 2011 article gave us its abstract. The later journal paper gives the explicit statement used here. [Journal Open Problem 2](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23011) · [Liu](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-30229-0_7) · [2011 publication record](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/42842/)

The closest construction we found is the planar nested-lozenge antichain. It contains a path of two-vertex rows with four pendant leaves. Each leaf has a different support vertex, and removing one support leaves the core connected. Any three leaves form an asteroidal triple. A permutation graph is asteroidal-triple-free: the middle of three pairwise disjoint segments separates the other two in the graph after removing its closed neighborhood. Thus the published nested lozenges lie outside the present target class. Their construction is credited in the proof. In the other published examples of Błasiok and coauthors, the alternating wheels have long induced rims, the Matoušek–Nešetřil–Thomas graphs contain triangles, the interval examples contain cliques, and the antiholes eventually contain triangles. [Earlier antichains and figures](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1510.07135#page=9)

The 2025 cubic bipartite antichain of girth six also lies outside the class, since a shortest cycle of length six is induced and bipartite permutation graphs exclude induced cycles longer than four. Lewchalermvongs studied a class excluding the four-wheel as an induced minor. Each of our examples contains an induced complete bipartite graph with three vertices on either side, and contracting one edge of that graph yields a four-wheel. The earlier result applies to a different family. [Cubic antichain, Appendix B](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.GD.2025.24) · [Lewchalermvongs](https://doi.org/10.31390/gradschool_dissertations.2224)

## Independent mathematical checks, 28 November 2026

The compatibility referee read the source, inspected the vector drawings of the earlier antichains, and checked the general common-neighbor argument. In particular, if the support intervals are separated, the four common neighbors need four distinct representatives in the same intervening row. Even endpoint branches that extend over more than one row must each meet the only neighbor of a host boundary row. Distinct internal layers cannot fold back onto one another because two such layers exceed the host capacity. The referee checked the two-internal-row case, reversal and the two special rows. A separate program agreed with the general criterion on 177 graph pairs, 79 negative, across 7,123 states produced using at most three deletion or contraction operations.

The statement referee compared the written proof, original question, Lean model, and the endpoint count. They separately enumerated literal deletions and contractions for 1,178 ordered host/target pairs with two to five internal rows. All comparisons agreed, including four direct tests of neighboring proposed antichain members. Both referees confirmed that the family addresses the bipartite permutation graph problem.
