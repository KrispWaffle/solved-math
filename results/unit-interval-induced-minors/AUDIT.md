## Opening search, 28 November 2026

Lozin and Mayhill asked whether finite unit interval graphs are well-quasi-ordered by induced minors. Liu records the conjecture with the bipartite permutation graph question in §3.2. Alecu, Lozin and Malyshev separately describe the unit interval question as open immediately before their Open Problem 2. The target here is the positive or negative answer for the entire unit interval class, including unbounded clique number and disconnected graphs. Comparisons allow arbitrary vertex deletions and edge contractions. Ding proves well-quasi-ordering for chordal graphs of bounded clique number and gives an induced-minor antichain of interval graphs. The bounds permit unbounded clique numbers in unit interval graphs, and the antichain uses intervals of different lengths whose graph contains a claw. Neither settles the present question. Lozin and Mayhill's canonical antichain concerns the induced subgraph relation. Błasiok and coauthors study a class defined by excluding a single induced minor among all graphs. The neighboring bipartite permutation result supplies an antichain for a different class. No proof or counterexample for the unit interval question appeared in this search.

The mathematical distinction is sharp enough to matter here. Variable-length interval graphs and bipartite permutation graphs both admit induced-minor antichains. The proposed approach studies what equal lengths do to separators. Intervals covering a point occur consecutively in start order. Coloring this order cyclically can carry several clique vertices through a region between two separators. The research question is whether these linkages combine with Higman's lemma to cover arbitrary clique sizes.

## Scope of the induced-minor relation

The journal discussion also asserts that unit interval graphs are closed under induced minors. Both reviewers independently identified the bull as a counterexample to that ancillary assertion. For an explicit representation take unit intervals starting at $-0.9,0,0.4,0.8,1.7$. Contract the edge between the vertices at $0$ and $0.8$. The resulting claw has three pairwise nonadjacent neighbors of its center. Three starts in the center's length-two neighborhood cannot have both consecutive gaps strictly greater than one, so the claw has no unit interval representation. The stated conjecture compares unit interval source and target graphs in the ordinary induced-minor relation. Its truth does not require closure of the class.
