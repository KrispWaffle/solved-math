# Unit interval graphs are well-quasi-ordered by induced minors

**Theorem.** In every infinite sequence of finite unit interval graphs there are two graphs, in their original sequence order, such that the earlier graph is an induced minor of the later graph. This answers the unit interval part of the conjecture of Lozin and Mayhill. The bipartite permutation part has a negative answer. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11083-010-9188-7) · [Liu, §3.2, Conjecture 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-30229-0_7) · [Bipartite permutation graphs](../bipartite-permutation-induced-minors/PROOF.md)

An induced-minor model assigns pairwise disjoint, nonempty, connected sets of host vertices to target vertices. Two distinct sets have an edge between them exactly when their target vertices are adjacent. This is equivalent to deleting vertices and contracting edges. Models compose by taking unions of the host sets assigned to each connected intermediate set.

## Dense stretches contain prescribed interval graphs

Represent each vertex by a closed interval of length one. We may assume that distinct left endpoints have differences outside the integers. Indeed, contract the coordinates of the left endpoints by a common factor sufficiently close to one, retaining unit lengths. All edges now correspond to distances strictly less than one and all nonedges remain at distances strictly greater than one. A small independent perturbation preserves these strict inequalities and avoids all integer differences. This also makes all start and end events distinct.

Order the intervals by their left endpoints, starting the indices at zero. The intervals covering a point have consecutive indices, because the same order sorts their right endpoints. If every point of a closed segment is covered at least $p>0$ times, coloring the indices modulo $p$ gives a cover of the entire segment in each color. The intervals of one color meeting the segment form a connected set in the intersection graph. Indeed their union contains the segment and each of them meets it. At a point covered exactly $p$ times each color occurs just once.

Fix any interval graph $H$ on $h>0$ vertices. Give it a representation with all endpoints distinct by ordering coincident starts before coincident ends and resolving ties among like events. Place its $2h$ endpoints, in that order, at positions $a+3,a+6,\ldots,a+6h$. These new intervals are called the target intervals. Suppose that the unit intervals of a host cover $[a,b]$ at least $h$ times everywhere and $b-a\ge 6h$. Assign each vertex of $H$ a different color modulo $h$. Its branch consists of all host intervals of its color meeting its target interval. Each branch is connected and nonempty. Distinct colors give disjoint branches. Intersecting target intervals have a common point covered by both colors. Disjoint target intervals are at distance at least three, so two unit intervals meeting them cannot intersect. Thus $H$ is an induced minor of the host. In particular, stretches of coverage at least $h$ in a host avoiding $H$ have length less than $6h$.

## Patches with ordered boundary cliques

Choose cuts between successive endpoint events or before or after all events. A patch on $[a,b]$, allowing $a=b$, consists of all intervals meeting this segment, with their intersection graph and the ordered arrays of intervals covering $a$ and $b$. The order in each array is by left endpoint. Every edge of the patch has an intersection witness in the segment. In fact, two intersecting intervals that both meet a segment have a common point in it. Patches on adjoining segments share precisely the intervals at their common cut.

A level-$p$ patch has $p$ intervals at each end and coverage at least $p$ between the cuts. An ordered model of one such patch in another is an induced-minor model in which each boundary vertex's branch contains the host vertex at the same position in the corresponding boundary array, on both ends. These models compose. At a boundary, every host port belongs to its required branch and no other branch meets that boundary there. All patches considered below come from representations of graphs avoiding the fixed $H$ as an induced minor.

At level $h$, all such patches are well-quasi-ordered by ordered models. Translate the left cut to zero. Every left endpoint of a retained interval now lies in $[-1,6h]$. Encode the intervals as a word in order of increasing fractional part of their left endpoints. A letter records the integer part and the position in each boundary array, with a separate symbol for absence. There are finitely many letters. The assumptions on differences make the fractional parts distinct. Two intervals in the same integer bucket are adjacent. In consecutive buckets they intersect exactly when the fractional part in the higher bucket precedes the one in the lower bucket. Intervals farther apart are disjoint. A letter-preserving subword therefore gives an induced subgraph embedding that preserves the ordered ports. Higman's lemma yields the assertion. [Higman, The ordering by divisibility in abstract algebras](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-2.1.326)

## Descending through the separator sizes

Assume that level $p+1$ patches are well-quasi-ordered by ordered models. In a level $p$ patch, consider the successive states of the ordered endpoint events, placing a cut in each intervening gap. Use the patch endpoints as the first and last cuts. Split the event sequence at every return to coverage $p$. Each resulting nonempty piece starts with a birth, ends with a death and has coverage at least $p+1$ in between. After trimming those two events, including the case of a single intervening state, it is a level $p+1$ patch with the same vertices and edges. The old left array is the first $p$ entries of the new left array, since the newborn interval has the largest left endpoint. The old right array is the last $p$ entries of the new right array, since the dying interval has the smallest right endpoint and hence the smallest left endpoint. An ordered model between the trimmed patches preserves the two old arrays as well.

For $p>0$, tag each nonempty piece by the residue modulo $p$ of the oldest interval at each of its two level-$p$ cuts, using indices in its ambient representation. Equal tags make the order-preserving correspondence of arrays preserve the individual colors as well. For $p=0$ use one trivial tag. The pieces with these tags are a well-quasi-order by the induction hypothesis and the finiteness of the tags. Higman's lemma now compares words of pieces by matching them to a subsequence with ordered models and identical tags. In an infinite sequence first restrict to patches of the same two *outer* tags, so that any initial and terminal gaps can also be traversed with the correct port order.

We detail how a matched subsequence gives an ordered induced-minor model. In each matched host piece use the supplied model. For every gap of skipped pieces between successive matches, the two corresponding source pieces meet at one level-$p$ cut. For each source vertex at this cut, extend its branch across the host gap by taking every host interval of its color that meets the gap. Each such track is connected, contains the required unique port at both ends and is disjoint from the other tracks. The tags ensure that these ports are exactly the two already assigned by the matched models. Attach initial or terminal gaps in the same way using the source's corresponding outer boundary clique. Identical outer tags ensure the final boundary orders. For $p=0$ there are no tracks to add. Two consecutive matches with a zero-length gap agree on the shared ports by the same rule.

To check this union of assignments, consider all the matched slabs and intervening gaps in their linear order. At every join their assignments agree on the entire shared boundary, exactly the host intervals present on both sides. A host interval appearing in several slabs is present at every cut between them. It consequently has just one owner wherever it is selected. This also covers an interval that belongs to both boundary arrays of a piece. Each source interval appears in consecutive source pieces, and its connected branch portions meet via their assigned ports and tracks. Thus the final branches are connected, nonempty and disjoint. Every target edge has a point of intersection in a source piece, whose matched model supplies a host edge. Conversely, overlapping selected host intervals have a witness in some retained slab or gap, including at a join. In a matched slab they are assigned by its induced model. In a gap all their owners are members of the one corresponding source boundary clique. Either case permits every resulting edge between different owners. All ordered outer ports are present. This proves the gluing claim.

A patch with no events consists of its single boundary clique with identical arrays at both ends. Such patches at fixed $p$ are all isomorphic as ordered patches. In an infinite sequence either such patches occur infinitely often or one may pass to patches with a nonempty word of pieces. This completes the induction down to $p=0$.

## The whole sequence

Suppose an infinite bad sequence exists, and take $H$ to be its first graph. An empty graph is an induced minor of every graph, so $h>0$. Every later graph avoids $H$. A unit interval representation of each later graph is a level-zero patch between a cut before all its starts and a cut after all its ends. The level-zero conclusion gives a comparable pair in the required order, a contradiction. Disconnected graphs and graphs of unbounded clique number are both included.
