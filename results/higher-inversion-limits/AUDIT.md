# Source, priority and review

## Question and previous results

Problem 8.4 of Claesson, Linusson, Ulfarsson and Verkama’s *Inversion monotonicity in subclasses of the 1324-avoiders*, version 2 of 6 November 2026, asks why each pair consisting of 1324 and another length-four pattern has a secondary or tertiary limit sequence. Section 5.2 specifies the differences and the predicted six-versus-five split. The trivial partner 1234 is treated separately. Verkama's thesis has the same question number and numerical evidence. The present result proves the split and gives an all-diagonal coefficient formula. Its general affinity theorem applies with any finite additional basis. [Primary source, §§5.2 and 8](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01143v2#S8) · [Thesis and numerical tables](https://diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2%3A2050045/FULLTEXT01.pdf)

At selection and again before publication on 28 November 2026, the author and reviewer searched by the statement number, secondary and tertiary limit sequences, diagonal first differences, the twelve symmetry representatives, and permutations with bounded inversion excess. The searches covered the primary paper and earlier version, newer citing papers, formal libraries, repository catalogues on both branches and public tracking issues. The primary source already proves the secondary formula for the partner 1342. Linusson and Verkama’s Theorem 8 gives the almost-decomposable structure used in the proof. Meng studies residual counts at $k=2n+O(1)$ for the unrestricted 1324 class. The arXiv and eScholarship records are versions of the same work. Neither these papers nor the later citing works examined gave the general finite-basis affinity theorem or explained the entire split. [Linusson and Verkama](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15075) · [Known partner, Theorem 7.14 in the PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.01143v2#page=35) · [Meng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06717) · [Deposited version](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/05s1r7jc) · [Shuffle automata](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02535) · [Inversion graph connectivity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17237)

The results concern fixed integer diagonals and eventual behavior in the length. They do not settle the separate inversion-monotonicity conjecture for the class of all 1324-avoiders. In particular, the negative slope for the partner 2143 gives an actual decrease of the difference along its diagonal. [Definitions and scopes](PROOF.md)

## The five tertiary series

The April version of the source and the thesis record five numerical series and conjectural relations for three of them. The revised article corrects the difference sign. The proof of the 4231 identity uses Franklín’s published one-end bijection with distinct-part partitions. The new result identifies the exact two-end count, its shift and its multiplicity. Searches before derivation and before publication for the individual partners, the generating functions, the printed chains of terms and subsequent citations found those predictions and one-end results. The searches identified no earlier proof of the five tertiary identities.
