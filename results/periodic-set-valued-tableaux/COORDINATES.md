# The coordinate convention changes the recurrence

Dal Zovo's v1 defines the order on cells by increasing both coordinates in equation (2), and defines the strip period on the next page by translating through $(1,-1)$. These two printed definitions give a superexponential number of ordinary tableaux. The cited Sun–Chan recurrence concerns the usual shifted strip in which, with both coordinates increasing in the order, the next row is shifted one cell to the right. The difference can be checked directly at every width and every fixed excess. [Primary PDF, pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.29520#page=5) · [Sun, strip convention](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.07256) · [Chan, periodic tableaux](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05594)

**Literal-coordinate theorem.** In a strip of $n\ge1$ translates of a column with $w\ge1$ cells, write the cells as $(i,k-i)$, where $0\le i<n$ and $0\le k<w$. This is the printed diagram up to a rigid translation. At every excess $e\ge0$ its number of admissible surjective words of length $wn+e$ is at least $(n!)^w$.

In the printed product order a strict comparison strictly increases the sum of coordinates, namely $k$. At each fixed $k$ all $n$ cells may therefore be listed in an arbitrary order, independently over the $w$ levels, while listing the levels in increasing order. The resulting words of distinct cells respect every comparison. Prepending $e$ more copies of the first cell gives admissible surjective words and preserves injectivity. This proves the inequality.

For every fixed $w$ and $e$ this bound eventually exceeds $a c^n$ for every positive $a,c$. Every sequence satisfying an eventual homogeneous constant-coefficient linear recurrence over the complex numbers is exponentially bounded, by induction using a bound on the sum of the absolute coefficients. Thus the fixed-excess counts in the printed coordinates cannot satisfy such a recurrence. This discrepancy is already visible at zero excess.

The same argument covers every nonempty finite motif $X$. Its translates through $(1,-1)$ preserve each occupied coordinate sum. On each of the $r$ represented sums, the translates of a single motif cell already give $n$ distinct cells. Independently permuting all cells on each level and listing levels in order gives at least $(n!)^r$ admissible words. Prepending repeats works at every fixed excess. Thus every literal periodic zigzag in v1 also fails eventual constant-coefficient recurrence as the number of translates grows.

For the mathematical question motivated by the cited tableau counts, use cells $(k,k+i)$ in diagonal $0\le i<w$, with the same product order and a period of $(1,1)$. This gives the usual shifted strip when the lower and upper profiles are constant across the diagonals. The arbitrary-profile extension in the [transfer proof](PROOF.md) uses this convention throughout.
