# Scope, prior work and independent review

Meng's Conjecture 6.7 states that *every* base residual permutation $\rho$ satisfies $\operatorname{inv}(\rho)\ge3|\rho|-15$. Equivalently its length is at most its own defect plus eight, where $\delta(\rho)=\operatorname{inv}(\rho)-2|\rho|+7$. It would bound the base objects that generate residual families beyond the known inversion-monotone range. The target includes all lengths and all defects. [Primary source, version 2, Conjecture 6.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06717v2#page=33)

A skeleton contracts every maximal increasing run of consecutive values in consecutive positions to one entry. A reduced permutation has no such bond. Start with a reduced sum-indecomposable 1324-avoiding skeleton $\sigma$ of length at least two. Look at the first position, last position and positions of the minimum and maximum. Among these positions, double exactly those whose deletion makes the skeleton sum-decomposable, doubling both when the length is two. Doubling replaces a single entry by an increasing block of two consecutive values and reindexes the alphabet. This is the source's base residual. In particular its length exceeds that of its skeleton by at most four. [Meng, Theorem 5.3 and Proposition 6.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06717v2#page=29)

The source's later uniform skeleton bound $|\mathrm{sk}(\pi)|\le2\delta+3$ is a different statement. Its finite catalogue and rank-three onset do not settle the global base inequality. The opening record identified the all-defect base inequality as the unresolved target. The quadratic skeleton estimate supplies the length cutoff in the proof. ## A nearby question settled by earlier results

Meng's Conjecture 5.26 asks for three vertices meeting at least $m-1$ inversion edges. This part follows from the 2007 path-domination theorem of Bacsó, Tuza and Voigt and Meng's own forbidden induced subgraphs. The other assertion is Meng's Theorem 5.29. In detail, the five obstructions in Bacsó–Tuza–Voigt's Theorem 1 are the six-vertex path, six-cycle, once-subdivided claw, and the triangle or four-cycle with one leaf at each vertex. Meng's Lemma 5.25 and the proofs of Theorem 5.27 and Lemma 5.28 exclude all five for the inversion graph in question. A connected dominating set of at most three vertices meets all the edges of a spanning tree. Independent source review also located the earlier three-vertex case cited by the 2007 authors. This is an application of prior results and is excluded from discovery counts. [Bacsó–Tuza–Voigt, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2005.11.035) · [Meng, pp. 25–27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.06717v2#page=25)

To see the extra pruning used by the left-to-right search, let $a$ be the minimum of the prefix. Its remaining entries larger than $a$ must avoid 213, since a 213 preceded by $a$ forms 1324. If there are $h$ such entries in $g+1$ value runs, extracting and standardizing them creates at most $g+a$ bonds with zero-based ranks. Each newly adjacent pair either spans a gap in their values, or has a smaller remaining entry between its positions. Only $a$ smaller entries are available. Contracting those bonds leaves a reduced 213-avoider of length at least $h-g-a$; contraction preserves inversions as a lower bound. Reverse-complementation identifies its minimum inversion weight with that for a reduced 132-avoider.

For completeness, split a 132-avoider at its maximum. Every value on the left exceeds every value on the right. For a nonempty right word of size $j$ the cross cost is $j(n-j)$, and the left word must be empty or end below its maximum to avoid a bond with the maximum. Write $u_n$ for the least weight when the word does not end in its maximum, and $v_n$ for the unrestricted least weight. Taking $u_0=v_0=0$ for an empty side gives $u_n=\min_{1\le j<n}(u_{n-1-j}+v_j+j(n-j))$ and $v_n=\min(u_n,u_{n-1})$. A minimum over an empty set is infinite. This recurrence supplies the second search’s additional lower bound. Its other pattern test keeps a ceiling for each remaining value $y$. Once a prefix contains $a,x$ in order with $a<y<x$, choosing $y$ while an unused value exceeds $x$ forces a 1324. Every 1324 is detected when its third position is filled.
