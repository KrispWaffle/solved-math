# Prior work and review

## Opening search, 28 November 2026

The source is the journal version of Alecu, Lozin and Malyshev, Conjecture 3, with its Definition 6 and the NP-hardness definition at the beginning of §5. This is distinct from Conjecture 2 on bipartite permutation graphs. The present question concerns all almost periodic words and asks for minimal hardness under taking hereditary subclasses. The theorem on minimal unbounded uniformicity in the same paper concerns a graph parameter, and its theorem on linear forests treats the constant fold. [Journal](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.23011) · [Preprint, Definitions and §5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.14467)

Searches covered “folded linear forests,” the authors and statement number, almost-periodic and Sturmian graph classes, enumerable reductions, minimal hard hereditary classes, and bounded uniformicity. The source, citing papers, public proof and issue catalogues, local branches and pinned Mathlib yielded no earlier solution of the journal conjecture in these searches. Brignall and Cocks use Sturmian words to distinguish graph classes for the different invariant of clique-width. Their construction uses a grid rather than a folded path. [Brignall–Cocks](https://doi.org/10.37236/10483) · [OpenAlex citing record of the source](https://openalex.org/W3095962291)

The possible distinction between an NP-hard problem and the absence of a polynomial-time algorithm is essential here. We study the former in the journal sense. Polynomial reductions are uniform and every output host must satisfy the restriction.

## Pre-publication search, 28 November 2026

We repeated the search for the exact target by author, paper number, DOI, conjecture number, folded path, minimal hard hereditary class, Sturmian host class, induced subgraph isomorphism and NP-intermediate hereditary restriction. We checked the original publication and preprint, their citation records, pinned Mathlib, local and remote problem branches, the repository issue and PR index and the reference repository tree. This bounded search found no earlier resolution of this journal conjecture. The journal defines the restricted problem on a promised host and defines hardness by NP-hardness. The polynomial many-one reduction used here is the usual uniform interpretation. The preprint instead calls a class hard when it lacks a polynomial algorithm. [Journal §4.1, Definition 6 and §5, Conjecture 3](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jgt.23011) · [Preprint §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.14467v1#page=16)

Amir and Hellouin de Menibus study computability of symbolic languages, including Sturmian languages. These works treat ingredients of the construction. None of them establishes minimal NP-hardness or its failure for the induced subgraph problem on the folded classes in this conjecture.
