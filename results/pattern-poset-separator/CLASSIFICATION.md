# A finite characterization of pattern-poset separators

Blitvić, Kammoun, and Steingrímsson ask which patterns have a separator in their fixed-overlap packing poset for more occurrences than the overlap size. The criterion below applies both to a specified occurrence count and to every such count at once. Its input consists of the pattern length and the ranks at the two overlapping ends. [Part I, §7, item 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29846v1#S7)

## The criterion

Let $p$ be a permutation of length $K$. Use ranks and positions $0,\ldots,K-1$. Fix $1\le m<K$ and set $d=K-m$. The two lists $P_i=p(i)$ and $S_i=p(d+i)$, for $0\le i<m$, must have the same relative order, as in the source. Window $t$ contains the physical positions $dt+i$ in the order of their ranks $p(i)$. The strict poset order is generated by these window orders. A separator is comparable to every position and has an incomparable pair strictly below it and another strictly above it. The poset is the ordinal sum of the strict lower set, the singleton separator, and the strict upper set. Either side can have several connected components. [Source definitions and overlap convention](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29846v1#S3) · [Poset construction](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29846v1#S5)

Define two maps on the chain $0,\ldots,K$, where $K$ is a sentinel.

```math
F(r)=\min\bigl(\{P_i:r\le S_i\}\cup\{K\}\bigr),
\qquad
G(r)=\min\bigl(\{S_i:r\le P_i\}\cup\{K\}\bigr).
```

For $n$ windows, form slots $(s,r)$ with $0\le s<n$ and $0\le r<K$. Define a comparison between two slots by

```math
(s,r)\preceq(t,q)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\begin{cases}
F^{t-s}(r)\le q & s\le t,\\
G^{s-t}(r)\le q & t<s.
\end{cases}
```

Write $T(n)$ for the following finite test. Choose a slot $z$ that compares with every slot. Among the slots weakly below $z$, require a pair incomparable in both directions. Require a second such pair among the slots weakly above $z$. The test succeeds if some choice of $z$ meets all three conditions.

**Theorem.** The packing poset on $j$ occurrences has a separator if and only if $T(\min(j,4K+1))$ succeeds. In particular, it has a separator for every $j>m$ if and only if $T(n)$ succeeds for each $n=m+1,\ldots,4K+1$.

The theorem characterizes the whole set of successful occurrence counts. Its tail is either empty or contains every count from $4K+1$ onward. The theorem makes no assertion about the number of separators in that tail. [Executable Lean statements](../../lean/SolvedMath/SeparatorClassification.lean)

## Proof

### Comparisons across windows

Starting at rank $r$ in one window, a weak comparison through shared position $i$ into the next window is possible when $r\le S_i$. Once there, every rank at least $P_i$ is reachable. Their union over eligible $i$ is the final interval starting at $F(r)$, with the sentinel representing the empty interval. Going left gives $G$ by exchanging the two lists. Induction on the distance constructs a path for each successful iterated comparison. At distance zero the whole path lies in the starting window.

To prove necessity, fix the starting slot $(s,r)$. In each row $t$, mark the final rank interval determined by the displayed comparison. For each shared index $i$, compatibility gives

```math
 F(u)\le P_i\ \Longleftrightarrow\ u\le S_i,
 \qquad G(u)\le S_i\ \Longleftrightarrow\ u\le P_i.
```

For example, the first inequality on the left gives some $h$ with $u\le S_h$ and $P_h\le P_i$. The common relative order gives $S_h\le S_i$. The converse follows by choosing $h=i$. The analogous argument proves the second equivalence. Consequently two consecutive rows agree on which shared positions are marked, on either side of the starting row.

For long overlaps a physical position can belong to several rows. Use coordinates $(t,i)$ for a row and a local **position**. The position representations $(t,d+i)$ and $(t+1,i)$ describe the same physical position. Their rank slots are $(t,S_i)$ and $(t+1,P_i)$. More explicitly, define marks in all rows $t\ge0$ by the same iteration. Repeating the identification normalizes any representation of $x$ to row $\lfloor x/d\rfloor$, local position $x\bmod d$, and rank $p(x\bmod d)$ without changing its mark. This defines a set of physical positions closed upward under every generating comparison in the finite stack. It contains the start. Any weak path, including one that leaves the interval between its endpoint rows, must therefore end in a marked slot. This proves the comparison formula. It also precludes a strict cycle, since reversing any strict generating edge would give a weak comparison from a higher rank to a lower rank in its own row. [Formal reachability and asymmetry proofs](../../lean/SolvedMath/PatternReachability.lean)

Every physical position has a slot representative, and every slot represents a physical position because $p$ is a permutation. Representatives of a single position compare in both directions. A pair of incomparable slots weakly below $z$ must therefore represent two distinct positions strictly below $z$; equality of either with $z$ would make the pair comparable. The same argument applies above $z$. Thus $T(j)$ is exactly separator existence in the original poset.

### Stability of three-slot comparisons

Both maps are nondecreasing on the $K+1$ ranks including the sentinel. For a nondecreasing self-map $f$ of this chain, comparison of $r$ and $f(r)$ makes the whole forward orbit either weakly increasing or weakly decreasing. There are at most $K$ strict moves. Once a step repeats a value every later step repeats it. Hence $f^a(r)=f^{\min(a,K)}(r)$. For fixed endpoint ranks, a comparison depends only on the two directed row distances capped at $K$.

Distinguish a row $s$ in $j$ windows. Its left and right extents are $s$ and $j-1-s$. Compare it with a row $S$ in another stack for the same pattern. Suppose each extent at $S$ is at least the corresponding extent at $s$ truncated at $2K$. Given any two other rows of the first stack, we can place them around $S$ while preserving all directed distances among the three rows up to the cap $K$.

Indeed, on each side walk outwards from $s$ to the chosen rows in order. Replace each consecutive gap $u$ by $\min(u,K)$. Each side has at most two gaps. Its new total is at most both its original extent and $2K$, so the new rows fit. The cap of a sum of gaps is unchanged by first capping the summands. This also preserves the distance between two rows on opposite sides of the center. Keeping their ranks therefore preserves every comparison among the three slots. This construction also permits coincident rows. [Formal distance and placement lemmas](../../lean/SolvedMath/WindowCutoff.lean)

When the two pairs of extents agree after truncation at $2K$, placement works in both directions. Given a separator slot in the first stack, use backwards placement to test its comparison with each slot in the second stack. Use forwards placement on a witnessing lower pair and again on a witnessing upper pair. The positions chosen for the two pairs can be different in these applications. This proves that the same rank in the matched row of the second stack also passes the separator test.

Finally let both stacks have at least $4K+1$ windows. If the center is less than $2K$ from the left end, keep that distance; both right extents are then at least $2K$. If it is less than $2K$ from the right end, keep the right distance. Otherwise choose the row at distance $2K$ from the left in the second stack. In each case both truncated extents agree. Thus $T(j)$ is constant for $j\ge4K+1$. Shorter lengths enter the test at their actual length. Combining this fact with the exact comparison formula proves both assertions. ## An example where the occurrence count matters

For $p=2143$ and $m=1$, the poset for two occurrences has a separator at the shared position, the fourth physical position in one-based indexing. With three occurrences, it has none. The finite test distinguishes the two counts. The checker also recovers the three and five physical separators in the two examples in the source.
