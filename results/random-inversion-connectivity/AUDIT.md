# Connectivity of a uniform random inversion graph

In §6 of his November 12 preprint, Emil Verkama asks whether a uniform permutation has an inversion graph whose vertex connectivity equals its minimum degree with probability tending to one. Vertices are positions, and two positions are adjacent if their entries occur in decreasing order. Connectivity is the least number of vertices whose deletion disconnects the graph, with the usual complete-graph convention. Proving equality with high probability would give the Rayleigh limiting distribution of connectivity from Bhattacharya and Mukherjee’s theorem for minimum degree. This is a question about all permutation orders as their size tends to infinity. [Verkama, §6, “Connectivity as a statistic”](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.17237v1#S6) · [Minimum-degree theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03582)

## Opening search on 28 November 2026

The search covered random permutation graphs and random inversion graphs, vertex separators and scanlines, superconnectivity, minimum-degree equality, and the Rayleigh limit. Bhattacharya and Mukherjee prove the limit for degrees. Gürerk, Işlak and Yıldız study isolated vertices and numbers of components. Acan and Pittel study connectivity in a permutation conditioned on its inversion number. These have different conclusions. Searches of this repository, the reference collection, external formalizations, public issues, and the source’s related results located no proof of this equality for the uniform model. The separate 351624-avoidance conjecture in the same source already has a public resolution. [Gürerk–Işlak–Yıldız](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.06678) · [Acan–Pittel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.0246) · [Prior resolution of the separate Conjecture 6.2](https://www.trureturing.org/research/verkama-2026-conjecture-62/)

The approach is to represent a cut by a pair of position and value thresholds. A small vertex cut must be near a diagonal corner. A non-singleton component behind a cut of size at most the minimum degree would force two permutation points to lie unusually close in both coordinates.
