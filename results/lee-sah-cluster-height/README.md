# Lee–Sah cluster-poset heights

Lee and Sah’s Conjecture 6.1 asks for a uniform limit shape for a random linear extension of a stack of chains. Fix integers $1\le a<b\le m$. Glue the element of rank $b$ in each of $n$ chains of size $m$ to the element of rank $a$ in the next chain. Let $X_0$ be the rank-$a$ element of the first chain, and $X_i$ the rank-$b$ element of chain $i$ for $1\le i\le n$. Let $f$ be the quantile function of the beta law with shape parameters $1+(a-1)/(b-a)$ and $1+(m-b)/(b-a)$. The conjecture says that, in a uniform extension, the maximum over $0\le i\le n$ of the difference in absolute value between the normalized rank of $X_i$ and $f((i+1)/(n+2))$ tends to zero in probability. The authors’ counting theorem gives the growth of the number of extensions. The conjecture concerns their individual ranks. [Primary source, Definition 1.3 and Conjecture 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.04921) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2018.10.001)

The written argument bounds the density of the entire spine by a polynomial times the density of ordered independent beta samples with one extra uniform coordinate. Exponential concentration of order statistics then survives normalization. Independent uniform order statistics on the full poset transfer the result to extension ranks. The argument covers every allowed choice of endpoints, including the first and last marked points. [Proof](PROOF.md) · [Search and verification record](AUDIT.md)

The written proof passed independent mathematical review. Lean now proves the exact law of the spine under conditioning on a legal extension, the inverse beta sampler and its product density, and the transfer from simultaneous continuous-label concentration to discrete extension ranks. The remaining formal step bounds deviations under that weighted spine law.
