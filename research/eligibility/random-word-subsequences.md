# Random-word subsequence concentration

**28 November 2026. Prior results cover the proposed discovery.** Biers-Ariel, Godbole and Kelley ask in §4(c) how tightly the number of distinct subsequences of a random word concentrates around its expectation. We screened independent letters on a fixed finite alphabet and stationary binary Markov letters, the models in their paper. This is distinct from their §4(b) question about permutations. [Original question and models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.08661#page=10)

The word-to-matrix correspondence makes existing probability theorems applicable. For a fixed word, let $c_i$ count its distinct nonempty subsequences ending in letter $i$. Appending $i$ replaces $c_i$ with one plus the previous total and leaves the other counts fixed. Żak gives the binary shear matrices explicitly and counts the empty word in the total. For an alphabet of size $d\ge2$, rescaling to $v_i=(d-1)c_i+1$ gives the matrices $T_i=I+e_i f_i^{\mathsf T}$, where $f_i(x)=\sum_{j\ne i}x_j$. The sum of entries of the resulting matrix product equals $(d-1)\sum_i c_i+d$. The count and the matrix norm therefore have logarithms differing by a bounded amount for nonempty input words. The source already gives the first-moment recurrence for each model. [Żak, Theorems 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00342#page=2) · [Source, §§3.2–3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.08661#page=7)

For independent letters with positive probabilities on their support, the shears are strongly irreducible. A finite invariant union of proper subspaces has maximal-dimensional components permuted by each shear. A common positive power fixes each such component, making it invariant under all $e_i f_i^{\mathsf T}$. The $f_i$ have zero common kernel. A nonzero component would contain a coordinate vector and hence every coordinate vector. A power of any one shear is unbounded in the projective linear group. Benoist and Quint's Theorem 1.1 thus supplies the nondegenerate Gaussian limit for the logarithm. Fair binary words also have a direct connection to the published central limit theorem for Stern's sequence. [Benoist–Quint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.09086#page=2) · [Bettin–Drappeau–Spiegelhofer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.05253)

For an irreducible stationary binary chain with a positive self-loop, return paths at that state include its shear $A$ and a product of the two distinct shears. The latter is positive and proximal. An invariant finite set of lines would be permuted by both generators, hence also by the two individual shears, which have different unique fixed lines. Cantrell and Sert's finite-state Markov product theorem gives a nondegenerate functional central limit theorem and exponential large deviations in the original time variable. A deterministic alternation has a fixed Fibonacci count at each length. Reducible stationary binary chains give constant words and a deterministic count. [Cantrell–Sert, Theorem 4.12](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms.12550) · [Żak, Proposition 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.00342#page=4)

In the nondegenerate cases the Gaussian limit already forces the typical exponential growth rate to be strictly below the expectation's rate. The finite first-moment matrix bounds the expectation by a polynomial times its exponential rate. Equality of the two rates would then make every positive fixed upper tail on the square-root scale tend to zero by Markov's inequality, contradicting the positive Gaussian tail. The exponential deviations theorem gives exponentially small probability of reaching a fixed proportion of the mean. The author and a separate source reviewer checked the recurrence, hypotheses and distinction between the word and permutation questions. The discovery catalogue receives no entry.
