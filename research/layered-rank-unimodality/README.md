# Rank-unimodality of composition ideals in generalized subword order

Vatter's Conjecture 2.6 asks whether the numbers of distinct compositions of successive weights that embed into any fixed composition form a unimodal sequence. An embedding is an increasing choice of host parts, each at least as large as the corresponding chosen part. Rank is the sum of the parts, including the empty composition at weight zero. This is exactly the principal-pattern question for layered permutations. A proof for all compositions or a principal host with a rank dip would settle the conjecture. [Primary statement, pp. 6–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16355v4#page=7)

## Opening search, 28 November 2026

Sagan's componentwise order fixes the positions of the parts and gives a different theorem. Chen proved the generalized-subword claim for every host with at most six parts and established tail log-concavity for certain bitonic hosts. Zhang and Zhang prove the one-descent and bitonic theorems, using a uniform-subword coupling. A further manuscript treats repetitions of an alternating two-valued word. The counterexample for unrestricted permutation intervals has a nonempty lower endpoint. The general conjecture remains open in the primary sources and in the checked formal libraries and catalogues. [Chen](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7385218) · [Zhang and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28619) · [Alternating family](https://aixiv.online/abs/2611.00543) · [Different interval conjecture](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/651)

I am studying whether every potential rank dip can be localized to a small interval around the midpoint, and how the rank differences behave there when the part sizes change. Choosing the leftmost embedding represents each distinct composition exactly once. Its allowed part sizes are independent integer intervals. This gives a way to prove a sign for the central differences and to distinguish a general resolution from a result for hosts with comparable part sizes. Searches used principal composition ideals, generalized subword order, greedy embeddings, layered permutations, rank differences, dilation, box sums and mode localization. I also checked the reference catalogue's main and research trees and issue records, the current repository and the related principal-rank work. [Related research on all permutations](../principal-pattern-rank-unimodality/README.md)

## What is proved

For each fixed number of parts, all sufficiently large hosts satisfy the conjecture, including hosts with arbitrarily small parts alongside large ones. There is an explicit threshold, so the problem for any specified number of parts is a finite decision problem. A separate criterion proves unimodality for hosts whose two largest parts cover the entire unresolved rank interval. [Fixed number of parts and two-largest-parts criterion](FIXED-NUMBER-OF-PARTS.md)

For every composition, the ranks weakly decrease starting at the integer part of half the total weight plus half the number of parts minus one half. Lean proves this on the actual sets of distinct subwords and verifies the unique greedy-box representation. When all layer sizes grow in positive, strictly balanced proportions, the written proof locates the peak at this rank, with at most one tie.
