# Scope and independent review

Vatter's Conjecture 2.6 quantifies over all finite compositions in generalized subword order and is equivalent to the layered principal-pattern case. This package proves a rank window for every host, an exact central-peak result for strictly balanced thick hosts, and eventual unimodality for all hosts with a specified number of parts. The explicit cutoff reduces each specified length to a finite computation. A proof for all numbers of parts and weights is still outstanding. [Conjecture 2.6, v4, p. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16355v4#page=7)

The proof author derived the disjoint greedy boxes, the common center of the full and one-omission terms, the polygon path bound for thick hosts, the two-largest-parts coefficient factorization, and an explicit multiscale cutoff for arbitrary parts. A separate Codex reviewer contributed the frozen-coordinate argument for thin parts and the extraction of the sharp finite degree interval in the two-largest-parts criterion. The author independently checked these steps, then gave the all-integer proof with uniform constants. The reviewer read the integrated manuscripts and approved the stated cutoff, all coefficient signs, the parity choices for diagonal lengths and the multiplicity comparison. A correction makes explicit that a central plateau can occur on the two-largest-parts side. ## Lean scope

The source model uses positive letters bounded by the capacities at an increasing selection of indices. The finite enumeration identifies repeated embeddings using equality of lists. Lean proves this is the embedding relation, that each word belongs to one greedy box, that the sum of box counts equals the actual rank count, and that the sharp upper-tail inequality holds for every host. The condition in the final theorem is exactly the integer form of starting at the floor of the stated center. The fixed-number-of-parts theorem has a reviewed written proof.
