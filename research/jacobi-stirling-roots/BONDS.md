# Recovering deletion rows from full Jacobi–Stirling permutations

Use the order $\bar1<1<\bar2<2<\cdots<\bar k<k$. A full word contains one barred and two unbarred copies of each letter. Everything strictly between equal unbarred letters is larger than that letter. Write $P_{k,i}(x)$ for the generating polynomial for internal descents after deleting exactly $i$ bars and summing over the deletion sets. [Original word model, Theorem 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1201.0622v2)

Say that label $j$ is active when $\bar j$ is immediately before the first $j$ or immediately after the last $j$. In a full word with an active label, move its bar to the other of these two positions. Both positions are legal. In a valid word, any equal-letter interval containing a $j$ in its interior has smaller label. Also the two copies of $j$ cannot straddle an endpoint of a larger equal-letter interval without lying entirely outside it or containing that whole interval. Moving the bar from one exterior position to the other thus preserves the defining inequalities. More directly, in either target gap an equal-letter interval strictly spanning the insertion contains the adjacent $j$, or ends or starts precisely there. Any interval that strictly spans the insertion and whose label differs from $j$ must have smaller label, since one of its interior entries is $j$. The new bar is greater than every such label. Its own equal pair still has the bar outside.

Moving one bar preserves the set of other active labels. Removing it could create a new bond only if its two neighbors formed another label's bar and matching unbarred letter. One of these neighbors is $j$, so that cannot happen. Insertion can destroy another bond only by inserting between its two letters. Again the target gap touches an unbarred $j$. For a chosen set of active labels, simultaneously removing their bars gives a unique underlying word. Reinsert a bar immediately before its first letter or after its last letter according to the chosen orientation. If two insertions use the same gap, put the bar attached to the letter on the left first. This proves that the choices of orientation are independent.

Choose the full words in which every active bar is on the left as canonical representatives. Let $d(w)$ be the number of internal descents of a representative and $b(w)$ its number of active labels. Then
```math
 \sum_{i=0}^k P_{k,i}(x)y^i=\sum_{w\ \mathrm{canonical}} x^{d(w)}(1+x+y)^{b(w)} .
```

Indeed, reinsert each missing bar on the left of its first unbarred copy and turn every active bar to the left. The canonical word now determines the given partial word if each active label is marked left, right, or missing. Removing a bar on the left preserves the descent count. Inserting it on the right adds one descent. All other comparisons agree because $\bar j$ and $j$ are consecutive in the alphabet and the other neighbor is a different letter (or outside the word). If both sides of a gap acquire bars for different labels, the comparison between those two bars has the same sign as the original comparison. This proves the identity, including the factors for simultaneous deletions. The empty word covers the base case.

In particular, if $J_k(x,z)=\sum_w x^{d(w)}z^{b(w)}$, then $P_{k,i}(x)=[y^i]J_k(x,1+x+y)$. This is an alternative exact expression for the equal-weight sum. Positivity of the required Turán expression after evaluating at negative $x$ remains to be shown.
