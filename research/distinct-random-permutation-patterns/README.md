# Distinct classical patterns in a random permutation

## Exact open questions and priority check, 28 November 2026

Let $\pi$ be uniformly random in $S_n$. Let $X_n$ count the distinct permutations obtained by choosing an arbitrary subset of positions in $\pi$ and replacing the chosen values by their ranks. Borrás-Serrano, Byrne, Godbole and Veimau ask whether $\mathbb E X_n/2^n$ has a limit and what it is. They also ask for the variance, the normalization and the limiting distribution of $X_n$. Counting the empty pattern changes the answer by one and has no effect on any of these limits. These are questions about all subsequences of a typical permutation. They differ from the classical extremal question and from the consecutive-pattern problem. [Original questions, v1 §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13194v1#S4) · [Authors' previous consecutive-pattern result](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.12179)

The authors withdrew their proposed lower bound of $2^{n-1}(1+o(1))$ in v2. They state that it is false and announce a future correction with a fifth author. The old near-perfect packing conjecture was already disproved by Féray's upper bound, strengthened with Godbole, as credited in v1. Our target is the exact limit and limit law asked for in §4. On 28 November I searched the paper's versions, the authors and potential follow-up, Féray's publication page, the Permutation Patterns 2025 program, earlier talks, variants including subpatterns, non-consecutive patterns and random permutation downsets, public GitHub issues, the reference catalogue and local branches and Mathlib. I found the withdrawn preprint and the earlier results for consecutive patterns and for extremal permutations. I did not locate a posted determination of the exact random classical-pattern limit. The announced correction warrants a further check before promotion. ## Research candidate

The reviewed written proof gives $e^{-2}$. It counts all equivalent embeddings of a random subset and shows that, with probability tending to one, they are precisely independent replacements of single points. For density $p$ the number of these replacements converges to a Poisson variable of mean $4(1-p)/p$. The fluctuations of $X_n/2^n$ itself come from close pairs in the permutation plot and give a nonconstant limiting product.
