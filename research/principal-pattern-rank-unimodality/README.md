# Rank-unimodality of principal permutation downsets

## Target and opening search, 28 November 2026

Vatter’s Conjecture 2.1 asks whether, for every finite permutation, the numbers of **distinct** classical patterns at successive lengths form a unimodal sequence. A pattern is obtained from any subset of positions by retaining the relative order of its values. Include the empty pattern and the whole permutation. A full proof must cover each host permutation, and a single host with a rank dip would disprove it. Murphy recorded the characteristic shape of principal downsets in 2003. Pouzet and Rosenberg already imply the increasing half up to the middle for every host. The open part is the possibility of additional changes of direction in the upper ranks. [Statement, history and first half, pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16355v4#page=4) · [Pouzet and Rosenberg](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195669886800075)

The general interval conjecture of McNamara and Steingrímsson is **already false**. The 25 November counterexample has a nonempty lower endpoint and does not settle the principal case. The 2026 source records ongoing work on layered permutations, and Yibo Zhang and Zhanhe Zhang treat one-descent composition ideals. I checked the current Vatter revision and its update section, cited papers, later reports, both branches of the reference catalogue, its issue and PR records, our current catalogue and the pinned Mathlib under permutation and pattern terminology. Searches with “principal order ideal”, “principal downset”, “rank sequence”, “typical random permutation” and “rank log-concavity” located no general resolution for principal ideals or theorem giving a single peak for asymptotically almost every host. [Prior interval counterexample](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/651) · [Its exact scope and Lean verification](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/658) · [One-descent ideals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28619)

## Approach

Study the rank sequence of a uniform host using the occurrence classes from the distinct-pattern theorem. A continuous limiting curve gives the large-scale shape but permits neighboring ranks to oscillate. To control actual adjacent ranks, approximate the normalized counts, to an error smaller than any prescribed inverse power, by the mean of a statistic depending on few positions. Nested subset sampling then bounds its second finite differences. The ordinary binomial coefficients have enough strict log-concavity to dominate that error. A probability tending to one would establish an asymptotic case of the conjecture. Exceptional hosts would still require a different argument. [Uniform length profile](../../results/distinct-random-permutation-patterns/UNIFORM-PROFILE.md)

## Reviewed research results

For every fixed positive sampling density below one, a uniform host has strictly log-concave ranks from that density to the top, simultaneously, with probability tending to one. Hence the conjecture holds for a proportion of hosts tending to one. With probability tending to one the peak is unique, and its integer displacement from the middle has an explicit limiting law for each parity. [Typical-host proof](TYPICAL-HOSTS.md) · [Peak location](MODE.md)

For arbitrary hosts the first-half theorem and the common-position reconstruction of Kaiser and Klazar yield a deterministic sufficient condition: a monotone interval of length at least the integer part of half the host length ensures rank-unimodality. The general conjecture remains open. [Monotone-interval criterion](LONG-MONOTONE-INTERVAL.md)

Lean verifies the exact second-difference bound for bounded statistics of any specified set of coordinates on the actual uniform slices, and the precise binomial curvature margin. It uses the standardized distinct-pattern counts to transfer normalized curvature into strict log-concavity and a unimodal rank sequence. The random-plot estimates and limiting law are written proofs. From the repository root, run `./scripts/verify.sh` locally.
