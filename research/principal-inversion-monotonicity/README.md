# Inversion monotonicity for the principal class avoiding 1324

Claesson, Jelínek and Steingrímsson conjecture that at fixed inversion count $k$, the number $a_n(k)$ of length-$n$ permutations avoiding 1324 is weakly increasing in $n$. This remains open. It would improve the upper bound for the Stanley–Wilf limit. Linusson and Verkama proved the assertion throughout $k\le2n-7$, and Meng obtained further regions near that line. The separate two-pattern question has an older answer using Schröder polynomials. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5736) · [Author's problem page](https://akc.is/inv-mono/) · [Linusson–Verkama](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15075) · [Meng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06717) · [Two-pattern prior-work audit](../../results/inversion-compatibility/AUDIT.md#the-separate-all-length-question)

On 28 November 2026 I checked the original paper, the latest revisions of the two low-inversion papers, the authors' public problem statements, the subsequent subclasses paper, and the earlier Schröder enumeration. Searches covered inversion monotonicity near the maximum, coinversions of 1324-avoiders, 4231-avoiders at linear inversion density, and entropy in sum-closed classes. These searches located proofs near the low-inversion boundary and the earlier two-pattern consequence. I found no general proof of the principal conjecture or a previous matching upper-end transfer in those sources.

The present approach looks near the *maximum* inversion count. Complement sends the source to the sum-closed class avoiding 4231. In those coordinates, keeping the original number of inversions when moving to length $n+1$ asks for exactly $n$ additional inversions. Direct sums allow independent blocks to supply this increase. [Upper-edge proof](UPPER-EDGE.md)

## Findings

At the low-inversion end, the conjecture holds eventually throughout every range `k = o(n² / log² n)`, including every power below two. The proof removes small dominating sets until an isolated vertex splits the remaining permutation into two partition-encoded classes. [Sparse-range theorem](SPARSE-INVERSIONS.md)

For every sufficiently large $n$ the conjectured count increases by an exponential factor whenever the inversion deficit $\binom n2-k$ is at most $3n$. More generally the proof gives an exact entropy criterion for any fixed linear strip in a sum-closed complement class. For 4231, a finite weighted block strictly extends the interval supplied by the unrestricted Lehmer bound. The intervening inversion counts and the full finite-length conjecture remain open. [Upper-edge proof](UPPER-EDGE.md) · [Exact strip rate and weighted criterion](ENTROPY.md)

Lean verifies the original fixed-$k$ inequality with an explicit exponential factor for every $n\ge1024$ and $64(\binom n2-k)\le n$. The underlying theorem applies to every family containing the reversals of all layered permutations. The wider entropy range has a written proof. [Finite proof and scope](FINITE-STRIP.md) · [Source declaration](PrincipalStrip.lean) · [Axiom audit](ProofAudit.lean) · [Local verifier](verify.sh)

An affirmative answer in every linear upper strip would force the length generating function of 1324-avoiders to be nonrational. The obstruction comes from the finite inversion density of a sum-closed class with a supercritical sequence of indecomposable blocks. [Conditional implication and proof](GENERATION.md)

On 28 November 2026 an independent mathematical reviewer checked the compactness and rounding argument, the direct-sum injection, the weighted-block criterion, and the distinction from prior lower-edge results. The reviewer independently derived the exact entropy rate and supplied the layered-class obstruction to extending the conclusion to all densities under the general hypotheses. The complete asymptotic argument is currently a written proof. [Prior lower-edge theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15075) · [Subsequent low-inversion regions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06717)

A second reviewer checked the original quantifiers, the tagged rank encoding, the available filler length, the explicit threshold, and the reversal to the same inversion count. They also independently checked the renewal obstruction and located the prior rationality and supercriticality theorems. Their bounded search located no earlier statement of the implication from CJS to nonrationality.

## Growing prefix restrictions

For the family in CLUV Conjecture 4.5, every fixed linear upper strip satisfies the exponential comparison uniformly while the restricted suffix occupies any fixed positive fraction of the positions. The prefix index can grow linearly with $n$. The proof uses the inversion weight of a value shuffle across one cut and a strict entropy estimate for bounded-area Dyck paths. An independent reviewer checked the argument and the exact entropy at the unrestricted endpoint. This result currently has a written proof. [Theorem and argument](CATALAN-PREFIX.md)

Meng’s companion manuscript treats the two ends of the spectrum at fixed complementary deficits. The upper-edge entropy result above allows the deficit to grow linearly with length. [Author’s source](https://github.com/lsmeng/1324-inversion-monotonicity/blob/c818d55a88489ae8e6e5e2b4a030742492e1c699/papers/two_ends.tex)

Two independent mathematical reviewers checked the sparse-range proof against Meng’s injection complement and the source graph hypotheses on 28 November 2026. One derived the reduction by deleting bounded neighborhoods of low-degree vertices. The other found the dominating-set iteration, which the first then checked independently. They checked the two staircase lengths, the early-isolate exception, the termination bound from an intermediate round, and uniform domination of the encoding cost. The same proof gives the relative asymptotic $a(n,k)/p_2(k)\to1$ in this range. The full sparse-range result currently has a written proof. [Proof and asymptotic](SPARSE-INVERSIONS.md)
