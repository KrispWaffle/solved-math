# What inversion monotonicity would imply for length enumeration

Write $D=\operatorname{Av}(4231)$, $a_n=|D_n|$, and let $W_n$ be the number of inversions of a uniformly random element of $D_n$. An affirmative answer to CJS Conjecture 13 would imply that the ordinary generating function of 1324-avoiders is **nonrational**. In fact the conclusion already follows from eventual monotonicity in *every* fixed linear upper strip. Both unrestricted questions are open. [CJS original statement](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5736) · [Bóna, Section 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08506#page=10) · [Bóna's 2024 problem report](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/4113/OWR_2024_06.pdf?sequence=4)

**A concentration obstruction.** More generally, suppose a family has $a_{n+1}/a_n$ bounded and $W_n/n\to\mu<\infty$ in probability. Fix any $0<\varepsilon<1/2$. For every sufficiently large $n$ at least one integer $j$ with $|j/n-\mu|<\varepsilon$ satisfies $b_{n+1}(n+j)<b_n(j)$, where $b_n$ is its inversion distribution. Indeed the source window contains $(1-o(1))a_n$ objects. Shifting the inversion count by $n$ puts the target density within $\varepsilon+o(1)$ of $\mu+1$. Its total mass is $o(a_{n+1})=o(a_n)$. Summing the opposite coefficient inequalities over the window would contradict these two estimates. Reversal identifies this comparison for 4231 with the fixed-$k$ comparison for 1324.

**Independent blocks.** Let $D$ be a sum-closed permutation class containing the singleton. Its members decompose uniquely into nonempty sum-indecomposable blocks. If $I(z)$ is their length series, $A(z)=\sum a_nz^n=1/(1-I(z))$. Suppose there is a positive $r$ with $I(r)=1$. Draw independent blocks, giving each individual indecomposable $\sigma$ probability $r^{|\sigma|}$. Write $L=|\sigma|$, $Y=\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)$. If both means are finite then, at the successive renewal times, cumulative inversions divided by total length converge almost surely to $\mu=\mathbb E Y/\mathbb E L$. All block sequences ending at length $n$ have the same probability $r^n$. Conditional on a renewal at $n$, their concatenations therefore have exactly the uniform distribution on $D_n$. The singleton gives span one. By the arithmetic renewal theorem the probability of hitting $n$ is $r^n a_n\to1/\mathbb E L>0$. The almost-sure ratio law at successive renewal times yields convergence in probability on conditioning. The unconditional probability of an atypical ratio at any renewal at or beyond $n$ tends to zero. Also $a_{n+1}/a_n\to1/r$. The preceding obstruction applies.

In particular it applies whenever the sequence of components is supercritical, so $I(r)=1$ at a point strictly inside the convergence disc. The block length then has an exponential tail, and $Y\le\binom L2$ has finite expectation. At a critical root the same conclusion holds if the weighted sum of inversions is finite. A length-$l$ indecomposable has at least $l-1$ inversions, so finite mean reward also forces finite mean length. [Madras–Troyka, sequence schema and asymptotics](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/articles/6178/download#page=13) · [CJS inversion bound for indecomposables](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5736#page=6)

**Rationality.** If $A$ is rational, then $I=1-1/A$ is rational. A nonpolynomial rational series with nonnegative coefficients and finite positive radius diverges at its positive radius, since that point is a pole by Pringsheim's theorem. A nonconstant polynomial $I$ also passes through 1 at a positive argument. In both cases $I(0)=0$ and $I(r)=1$ at an interior point. Thus rationality forces supercriticality, also Bóna's Theorem 6.3. Applied to $D$, rationality would force a violation in a fixed linear upper strip at every sufficiently large length. Reversal preserves the length counts of the two principal classes.

The same argument for every forbidden pattern beginning at its minimum and ending at its maximum would give Bóna's remaining principal nonrationality conclusion from CJS Conjecture 20. For length at least three, the identity patterns are covered by Bóna's published theorem, as are patterns whose endpoints do not consist of the two extrema. Reversal deals with the opposite orientation of the extrema. This implication supplies a consequence of full inversion monotonicity. Establishing the hypothesis for every strip, or proving nonrationality unconditionally, remains a separate mathematical problem. [Bóna's Theorems 4.3 and 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.08506#page=8)
